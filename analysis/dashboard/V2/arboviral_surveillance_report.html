Paragraph 3 — Enhanced Surveillance Recommendations: Based on the concordance tier distribution, the following tiered surveillance actions are recommended. For counties at HIGH or CRITICAL risk: issue a Health Alert Network (HAN) physician advisory requesting arboviral serology (dengue NS1 antigen + IgM/IgG ELISA; chikungunya IgM; Zika PRNT) for all fever+rash patients with plausible exposure history; intensify Aedes aegypti trap density by 50% in affected postal codes with twice-weekly retrieval; and initiate targeted larval source reduction campaigns. For counties at MODERATE risk: transition from passive to enhanced passive surveillance (clinician education, flagging, and follow-up contact for all new EpiHack fever+rash reporters) and maintain ArboNET reporting cadence. Statewide: convene a joint ADHS/county health arboviral teleconference to review current-season baseline data and activate the Arizona Vector-Borne Disease Contingency Plan if 5 or more counties remain at HIGH+ tier by the next 30-day update cycle.

Paragraph 4 — Critical Data Gaps: This assessment is subject to four critical data limitations. First and most significant, EpiHack currently lacks a travel history field: without knowing whether reporters traveled to dengue- or Zika-endemic regions within 14 days of illness onset, it is impossible to distinguish imported cases — the dominant introduction pathway for Aedes-borne viruses in AZ — from locally-acquired transmission, which would represent a qualitatively different public health threat. Second, mosquito trap positivity data in this report derive from a deterministic seasonal simulation calibrated to Great AZ Mosquito Hunt historical data rather than current-cycle field observations; real-time integration of county trap-positivity feeds is required for operational decision-making. Third, Arizona lacks a statewide tick drag surveillance program with real-time data availability, creating a blind spot for Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever (RMSF), which is AZ-endemic and presents with an indistinguishable fever+rash syndrome requiring immediate doxycycline — a clinically critical differential that tick surveillance data would allow this system to assess simultaneously. Fourth, ArboNET case linkage to EpiHack reporter postal codes is not currently established; building a de-identified spatial join between ADHS MEDSIS ArboNET records and EpiHack hot zones would substantially improve early outbreak detection sensitivity.
